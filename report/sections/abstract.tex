\begin{abstract}
I build an end-to-end streaming pipeline that replays real telemetry from the Marconi100 supercomputer through Kafka, aggregates it with Spark Structured Streaming into TimescaleDB, and scores nodes with pluggable online detectors. The pipeline sustains 74\,k messages/s with a median end-to-end latency of 3.3\,s and loses no data across a mid-run restart. Using 146 nodes over 2.5 years, I study two tasks against the Nagios label ``node drained by the scheduler'': detecting the current state and anticipating drains. Generic unsupervised detectors perform poorly (best per-node AUC 0.59). Drained nodes are mostly idle (73\% of drained windows against 6\% of normal ones), but idle nodes are rarely drained (21\%). A single feature chosen on training data reaches 0.82 AUC and a supervised model 0.88, yet precision stays near 33\%. For anticipation one hour ahead, a gradient-boosted model learned from delayed labels and retrained every four weeks matches the best single-feature rule in AUC but raises about four times fewer false alerts at equal recall, while drift-triggered retraining does not beat a fixed schedule.
\end{abstract}
